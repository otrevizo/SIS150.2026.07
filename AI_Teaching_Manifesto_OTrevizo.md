# Oscar Trevizo’s AI Teaching Manifesto

*Responsible Integration in Teaching, Research, and Administration*

I believe AI belongs in the classroom, research, and administrative work of teaching, but only on my terms. This is what I commit to.

## 1. AI as Accelerant, Not Authority

- AI generates syntax, suggests libraries, and catches typos. I own the logic, the sequencing, and teaching intent.
- Every AI output gets reviewed the way I review any rough draft: Line by line, before it reaches a student, colleague, or record.
- AI supports learning. It does not replace scholarly effort.
- No student PII or confidential data goes into unapproved AI tools.
- No grades or feedback are issued by AI: Grading remains my own judgment applied with school's standard tools.
- No AI-drafted material is presented as original scholarship without disclosure.
- Follow school's data governance and academic integrity policies.

## 2. Teaching Students to Think, Not Paste

- Fundamentals come first: Variables, control structures, data structures. No shortcuts.
- AI-assisted coding without that foundation produces bad results: Code that runs but that the student can't explain, debug, or extend.
- Once the foundation is solid, I teach AI-assisted coding, vibe coding, explicitly, as its own skill.
- Students may use AI for syntax help and debugging. They may not submit a line of code they can't explain.
- Copying AI output without understanding it carries the same risk as plagiarism.
- The same standard applies beyond code: In discussions and papers, students must explain and defend their own reasoning, whether or not AI helped draft it.
- Assignment design matters as much as intent. Loosely scoped, open-ended prompts invite long, copy-pasted AI responses. Precise, narrow questions produce shorter answers that show real reasoning.
- I write tighter prompts for graded discussions. If a question is too open to answer honestly without AI, that's my failure to fix, not the student's failure to resist.

## 3. Pilot Before Scale

- This term: A live-coding demonstration for SIS150 Module 5 on inheritance.
- AI handles syntax and boilerplate. I own the class structure and learning objectives.
- I narrate in real time where AI assistance ends and my own design decisions begin.
- If it works, I extend the pattern to two or three more modules next term.

## 4. Where I Will Be Growing

- Student AI experience grows year over year, so I stay ahead of trends and read where each student stands.
- Build toward multimodal, human-centered AI, neurosymbolic; i.e., assisted across every channel, judged by me, case by case, not on autopilot.
- Workflow growth: Expand AI use-cases beyond vibe coding and graded discussions.
- Assessment: Telling genuine understanding from well-prompted AI output, in code, discussion, or a paper.
- Prepare for the unknown: I don't know what the classroom looks like in five years, so I keep adapting.
